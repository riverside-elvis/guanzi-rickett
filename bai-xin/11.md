## [XI]

天之視
Through the observation of Heaven,
而精
discover your vital essence. (精 *tsjieng*)

四辟[^bai-xin-62]
Through the movement of the four seasons,
而知情[^bai-xin-63]
come to know your inner reality. (情 *dzjieng*)

[^bai-xin-62]: Emending 四璧 to 四辟 (闢), lit., the four openings [Ding Shihan and Zhang Peilun].

[^bai-xin-63]: Emending 請 to 情 [Ding Shihan].

壤土
Through the rich earth,
而與生
find sustenance for your life. (生 *sreng*)

能若夫風與波乎
Can you be like the wind and waves,
唯其所欲適
desiring only what is appropriate to the circumstances?

故子而代
Now when a son takes the place of his father,
其父曰義也
he is said to be righteous;
臣而代
when a minister takes the place of his prince,
其君曰篡也
he is said to be rebellious.[^bai-xin-64]
篡何能歌
How can rebellion be praised in song?
武王是也
King Wu was an example of this.[^bai-xin-65]

[^bai-xin-64]: According to the _Zhuangzi_, VI, 17/10al12-13 (Watson, _Chuang Tzu_, 181):
“Emperors and kings had different ways of abdicating their thrones;
the three dynasties had different rules of succession.
Those who went against the times and flouted custom were called usurpers;
those who accorded with the times and adhered to custom were called companions of righteousness.”
The text then goes on to say: “From the point of view of the Way, what is noble or what is mean?
These are called ebb and flow.”

[^bai-xin-65]: According to the _Shang shu dazhuan_ (_SBCK edition_), 3/1b9-10:
“When King Wu attacked Zhou 紂 [of the Shang], on reaching the suburbs of the Shang capital,
he stopped to rest for the night. The troops were all delighted,
singing and dancing while waiting for the dawn.”

故曰
Therefore it is said:

庸能去辯與巧
Who is it that is able to dispense with bickering and trickery (巧 *krəw*)

而還與眾人同道
And return to sharing with ordinary men the same road? (道 *dəw*)

故曰
And again:
思索精者
“As thought and inquiry are refined,
明益衰
their brilliance increasingly declines;
德行修者
as virtuous conduct is cultivated,
王道狹
the kingly way becomes more narrowly circumscribed;
臥名[^bai-xin-66]利者
as one becomes content with fame and profit,
寫生危
one’s life is placed in peril.”
知周於六合之內者
I know that once my knowledge has come to encompass all within the six directions,[^bai-xin-67]
吾知生之有為阻也
my life will be beset by obstacles.

[^bai-xin-66]: Guo Moruo would emend 臥名 to 頤名 in order to preserve syntactic balance
with the two preceding lines. Thus, “As one becomes skilled in argument,
one’s life is placed in peril.”

[^bai-xin-67]: The six directions (*liu he* 六合) are north, south, east, west, above, and below.

持而滿之
To grasp life and live it to the full
乃其殆也
will then be fraught with danger. (殆 *dəy*)

名滿於天下
Before one’s fame has spread throughout the realm,
不若其已也
it is best to stop. (已 *riəy*)

名遂[^bai-xin-68]而身退
To achieve fame and then withdraw
天之道[^bai-xin-69]也
is Heaven’s way. (道 *dəw*)

[^bai-xin-68]: Emending 進 to 遂 [Zhang Peilun and Xu Weiyu] in accordance with a similar statement in
the Heshanggong 河上公 (_SBCK_) edition of the _Laozi_, A, 9/4b6: 功成名遂身退，天之道,
“To accomplish one’s tasks, achieve fame, and then withdraw is the way of Heaven.”
The Wang Bi 王弼 (_SBBY_) edition, A, 9/5a (Waley, _The Way and Its Power_, 152), writes:
功遂身退，天之道, “To accomplish one’s tasks and then withdraw is the way of Heaven.”
The _Laozi_. “A” and “B” texts (_Wenwu_, 1974, no. 11: 12, 18) concur with the Wang Bi version.

[^bai-xin-69]: Irregular rhyme: 道 (group 幽) with 已 (group 之), above.

滿盛之國
In a state that has reached its peak,
不可以仕任[^bai-xin-70]
one should never accept office. (仕 *dziəy*)

滿盛之家
Into a family that has reached its peak,
不可以嫁子
one should never marry one’s daughter. (子 *tsjiəy*)

驕倨傲暴之人
With men who are arrogant and domineering,
不可與交[^bai-xin-71]
one should never associate. (交 *kraw*)

[^bai-xin-70]: Irregular rhyme: 交 (group 宵) with 子 (group 之), above.
A close parallel to these three lines is to be found in the Mawangdui _Cheng_ text, 90.
