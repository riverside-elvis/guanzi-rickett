## [V]

庸能治[^bai-xin-33]無治乎
Who is it that is able to establish order while not establishing order, (治 *diəy*)

[^bai-xin-33]: Emending 法 to 治, both here and below [Guo Moruo].

始無始乎
Initiate affairs while not initiating them, (始 *st’iəy*)

終無終乎
Bring them to a conclusion while not bringing them to a conclusion, (終 *tjəwng*)

躬[^bai-xin-34]無躬乎
Be personally involved while not being personally involved? (躬 *kjəwng*)

[^bai-xin-34]: Following Guo Moruo in emending 弱 (*njawk*), “weak,”
here and at the end of the sentence, to 躬 in order to preserve the rhyme.

故曰
Thus it is said:

美哉
How beautiful the sage,
岪岪
rising like a mountain.

故曰
Thus it is also said:

不[^bai-xin-35]中
Without seeking the mean,
有中[^bai-xin-36]
he achieves it. (中 *tiəwng*)

[^bai-xin-35]: Emending 有 to 不 [Yu Yue and Dai Wang].

[^bai-xin-36]: Wang Niansun would emend 有中有中 to 中有有中.
Thus: “Within the center there is another center.”
This would accord with a somewhat similar statement appearing in the “Nei ye,” 3b11 (101.140):
心之中又有心, “Within the mind there is another mind.”

庸能得夫中之衷乎
Who is it that is able to grasp what lies within this mean? (中 *tiəwng*)

故曰
And further:

功成者隳
Success once achieved may be destroyed; (隳 *xiwa*)

名成者虧
Fame once achieved may be lost. (虧 *k’iwa*)

故曰
And again:

孰能棄名與功
Who is it that can reject fame and success (功 *kewng*)

而還與眾人同
And return to being like one of the masses? (同 *dewng*)[^bai-xin-37]

[^bai-xin-37]: These two quotations are very similar to the _Zhuangzi_,
VII, 20/11b9-10 (Watson, _Chuang Tzu_, 214): “I once heard a man of great accomplishments say,
‘Those given to boasting will have no success. Success once achieved may be destroyed;
fame once achieved may be lost. Who can rid himself of success and fame
and return to join the masses of men?’”

庸能棄功與名
Who is it that can reject success and fame (名 *mjieng*)

而還反無成
And return to having no accomplishment? (成 *djieng*)

無成
Those with no real accomplishments
貴其有成也[^bai-xin-38]
honor having accomplishments. (成 *djieng*)

[^bai-xin-38]: The meaning of this and the following sentence is not at all clear.
I have followed Wang Niansun, who would emend the first sentence, 有成無貴其成也,
to read 無成貴其有成也, in accordance with the phraseology of the following sentence.
Guo Moruo, on the other hand, would leave the first sentence as is and emend the second sentence,
有成貴其無成也, to read 有成無貴其成也.
Thus: “Having no accomplishments is honored as an accomplishment.
Having accomplishments is not honored as an accomplishment.”

有成
Those having real accomplishments,
貴其無成也
honor having no accomplishments. (成 *djieng*)

日極
As the sun reaches its peak,
則仄
it begins to set;
月滿
as the moon becomes full,
則虧
it begins to wane.
極之徒仄
Things reach their peak only to decline,
滿之徒虧
become full only to shrink,
巨之徒滅
and become great only to be destroyed.

庸能己忘己乎[^bai-xin-39]
Who is it that can make himself forget himself, (己 *kiəy*)

[^bai-xin-39]: The Yang edition for 己無已 writes 已無已. I have followed Wang Niansun and Yasui,
who would read 已 as 己 and interpret the sentence in accordance with the Yin Zhizhang commentary,
emending 無 to 亡 = 忘.

效夫天地之紀
To follow Heaven and Earth’s guiding principle? (紀 *kiəy*)
