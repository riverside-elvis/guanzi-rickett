## [XII]

道之大如天
The Way is vast like Heaven,
其廣如地
broad like Earth,
其重如石
heavy like stone,
其輕如羽
and light like a feather.
民之所以
It is something all people use,
知者寡
but few understand.
故曰
Therefore it is said:

何道之近
How can the Way be so close,
而莫之[^bai-xin-72]能服也
and yet there be no one able to apply it? (服 *bjwək*)

[^bai-xin-72]: Deleting 與 in accordance with the Ancient edition [Yasui, Xu Weiyu, and Guo Moruo].

棄近而就遠
Why do people, by ignoring its immediate presence and seeking it afar,
何以費力也
continue to waste their energy? (力 *liək*)

故曰
Therefore I say:

欲愛吾身
If I wish to care for my body,
先知吾情[^bai-xin-73]
I must first come to understand my inner reality. (情 *dzjieng*)

[^bai-xin-73]: For a discussion of *qing* 情 and other terms appearing in this paragraph,
see my introductory comments.

周視[^bai-xin-74]六合
For this, I must observe everything within the six directions
以考內身
as a basis for examining what lies within the self. (*st’jien*)

[^bai-xin-74]: Emending 君親 to 周視 in accordance with the Yin Zhizhang commentary [Yu Yue and Xu Weiyu].

以此知象
Having, in such a way, come to recognize my personal qualities,
乃知行情
I may then come to understand how to manage my inner reality. (情 *dzjieng*)

既知行情
When one understands how to manage one’s inner reality,
乃知養生
one may then come to understand how to nurture one’s life. (生 *sreng*)

左右
Turn[^bai-xin-75] to left and right,
前後
before and behind,
周而復所
and return to the original position. (所 *siay*)

[^bai-xin-75]: The following paragraph appears to be a discussion of some form of yoga practice,
but the text is extremely difficult to interpret since we know so little about the method involved.

執儀
Hold fast to discipline,
服象
suppress your personal qualities,
敬迎來者
and respectfully welcome whatever comes. (者 *tjiay*)

今夫來者
Now whatever comes,
必道其道
you must be certain to follow the proper way. (道 *dəw*)

無頡無衍
Do not change or add to it,
命乃長久[^bai-xin-76]
and your life then will be preserved for a long time. (久 *kjwəg*)

[^bai-xin-76]: Irregular rhyme: 久 (group 之) with 道 (group 幽), above.

和以反中
Keep yourself in harmony in order to return to the mean,
形性相葆
and your form and basic nature will then nurture each other. (葆 *pəw*)

一以無貳
Concentrate your mind and never divide your attention---
是謂知道
such is called coming to know the Way. (道 *dəw*)

將欲服之
Should you wish to suppress [your personal qualities],
必一[^bai-xin-77]其端[^bai-xin-78]
you must concentrate on fundamental feelings
而固其所守[^bai-xin-79]
and be steadfast in preserving them. (守 *st’jew*)

[^bai-xin-77]: The Yang edition mistakenly omits 一 after 必 [Guo Moruo].

[^bai-xin-78]: The meaning of *duan* 端, which I have translated here as “fundamental feelings,”
is not entirely clear. I have based my translation on the _Mengzi_, IIA, 6/5-7,
which speaks of the four *duan* or feelings, which are the beginnings of virtue:
“The feeling of commiseration is the beginning of humaneness.
The feeling of shame and dislike is the beginning of righteousness.
The feeling of modesty and yielding is the beginning of propriety.
A sense of right and wrong is the beginning of wisdom.”
However, it is also possible that what is being referred to here is a primal breath.
Thus this sentence might read:
“Should you wish to suppress [your personal qualities],
you must concentrate the primal breath and be steadfast in preserving it.”

[^bai-xin-79]: Rhyme: 守 (group 幽, 2nd tone) with 道 (group 幽, 3rd tone), above.

責其往來
We seek its going and coming, (來 *ləy*)

莫知其時
But no one can tell when this will be. (時 *djiəy*)

索之於天
We must search for its signs in Heaven
與之為期
to discover its natural time of arrival. (期 *kiəy*)

不失其期
By grasping its natural time of arrival,
乃能得之
we will then be able to succeed. (*tjiəy*)

故曰
Therefore I say:
吾語若大明[^bai-xin-80]之極
“My words are like the quintessence of the Great Brilliance.
大明之明非薆人
The brilliance of the Great Brilliance is not concealed from men,
不豫[^bai-xin-81]也
it is just that they are not prepared for it.
同
If a person is in accord with the Great Brilliance,
則相從
it will come of itself.
反
If a person is not in accord with the Great Brilliance,
則相距也
it stays away.”
吾察反
I have investigated this “not being in accord”
則相距
and the resulting “staying away.”
吾以故知古之從[^bai-xin-82]同也
Therefore, I understand how the ancients brought themselves into accord with the Great Brilliance.

[^bai-xin-80]: The Yin Zhizhang commentary explains the “Great Brilliance” 大明 as Heaven.
It may refer to a specific heavenly body. Yasui says it refers to the Way.

[^bai-xin-81]: Reading 愛 as 薆 and 予 as 豫 [Guo Moruo].

[^bai-xin-82]: Following Ding Shihan and Yao Yonggai, who would invert 從之 to read 之從.
Guo Moruo would emend 古 to 同. Accordingly: “Therefore, I understand the meaning of ‘being in accord.’”
