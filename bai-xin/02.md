## [II]

原始計實
The sage goes back to the beginning to gauge the reality of things
本其所生
and determines the base from which they have sprung. (生 *sreng*)

知其象
Through coming to understand their personal qualities,
則索其性[^bai-xin-12]
he examines their basic nature. (性 *geng*)

[^bai-xin-12]: Emending *xing* 形, “form,” to *xing* 性. The Yang edition writes *xing* 刑, “punishment.”

緣其理
Through determining their inherent structure,
則知其情
he comes to understand their inner reality. (情 *dzjieng*)

索其端
Through examining their beginnings,
則知其名
he comes to know their names. (名 *mjieng*)

故苞物眾者
Now, many things may encompass other things,
莫大於天地
but none are greater than Heaven and Earth.
化物多者
Numerous things may cause transformations,
莫多於日月
but none more so than the sun and moon.
民之所急
Of those things about which people worry,
莫急於水火
none are more pressing than water and fire.
然而天不為一物枉其時
Even so, Heaven does not alter its seasons for anything.
聖人[^bai-xin-13]亦不為一人枉其法
The sage does not bend his laws for anyone.

[^bai-xin-13]: Deleting 明君 before 聖人 as a later interpolation [Ding Shihan].

天行其所行
Heaven carries out its functions,
而萬物被其利
and all things are benefited.
聖人亦行其所行
The sage also carries out his functions
而百姓被其利
and the hundred surnames are benefited.
是故
For this reason,
萬物均
all things are treated equally
百姓[^bai-xin-14]眾矣
and the hundred surnames proliferate.

[^bai-xin-14]: Emending 既誇 to 百姓 in order to preserve the balance with the preceding sentence.
Yin Tongyang would read 均既 as 均概. Thus: “For this reason,
all things are treated equally and allowed to proliferate.”

是以聖人之治也
Such being the case, the sage, in maintaining good order, (治 *diəy*)

靜身以待之
Remains quiescent while waiting for things. (*tjiəy*)

物至
When things arrive,
而名[^bai-xin-15]之
he assigns names to them. (之 *tjiəy*)

[^bai-xin-15]: Deleting 自治 as an interpolation taken from the following line [Tao Hongqing].

正名
Their names being correct,
自治之
he automatically establishes good order among them. (之 *tjiəy*)

奇名
Were the names distorted,
自廢之[^bai-xin-16]
he would automatically reject them. (之 *tjiəy*)

[^bai-xin-16]: Emending 奇身名廢 to 奇名自廢 [Wang Niansun]
and inserting 之 after 廢 in order to complete the rhyme.
The passage IV, 12/13a 1-2 (1:57.1) states: “If terms (names) are correctly adhered to,
there will be good order; when they are distorted, disorder prevails.
If there is no terminology at all, death ensues.”
The “Lun” 論 section of the Mawangdui _Jing fa_ text (p. 29) contains a similar statement.

名正法備
When names are correct and laws are complete, (備 *biwəy*)

則聖人無事
The sage has nothing left to do. (事 *dziəy*)

不可常居也
The sage cannot always maintain a fixed position, (居 *kjay*)

不可廢舍也
Nor can he easily abandon his ground. (舍 *st’iay*)

隨變斷事也
He must accord with change in deciding matters (事 *dziəy*)

知時以為度[^bai-xin-17]
And understand the times in making assessments. (度 *day*)

[^bai-xin-17]: Irregular rhyme: 度 (group 魚) with 事 (group 之), above.

物有所餘
Things may be plentiful
有所不足
or in short supply. (足 *tsjew*)[^bai-xin-18]

[^bai-xin-18]: Inverting the order of these final two sentences: 物有所餘，有所不足 and 大者寬，小者局.

大者寬
When there is plenty, he may be generous,
小者局[^bai-xin-19]
but during times of scarcity, he must exercise tight control. (局 *gjewk*)

[^bai-xin-19]: Rhyme: 局 (group 侯, 4th tone) with 足 (group 侯, 3rd tone), above.
