## [IV]

道者一人用之
Even if just one person were to use the Way,
不聞有餘
one would never hear of there being any left over.
天下行之
Even if the entire realm were to carry it out,
不聞不足
one would never hear of there not being enough.
此謂道矣
This is what we call the Way.

小取焉
When a little of it is grasped,
則小得福
there is some prosperity; (福 *pjwak*)

大取焉
When a great deal is grasped,
則大得福
there is great prosperity. (福 *pjwak*)

盡行之
When it is carried out to the full,
而天下服
the entire realm submits. (服 *bjwək*)[^bai-xin-26]

[^bai-xin-26]: Cf. XV, 42/2b4-5 (2:86.8-9): “Those who grasp a little are benefited some;
those who grasp a great deal are benefited greatly.
Those who carry it out to the full will possess the entire realm.”

殊無取焉
If a ruler absolutely refuses to grasp it,
則民反
his people will revolt,
其身不免於賊[^bai-xin-27]
and he himself will be unable to avoid great harm. (賊 *dzək*)

[^bai-xin-27]: The following short passage,
左者出者也，右者入者也，出者而不傷人，入者而自傷也,
appears to be a misplaced slip, which I have moved to 9b2 (69.12).

不日不月
The sage never relies on the positions of the sun and moon [to pick lucky dates],
而事以從
but his undertakings are smoothly successful. (從 *ts’jewng*)

不卜不筮
He never resorts to divination by tortoise shell and milfoil,
而謹知吉凶
yet he is skilled in foretelling good and bad fortune. (凶 *xjewng*)[^bai-xin-28]

[^bai-xin-28]: Cf. the “Nei ye,” 5a2 (103.1), and “Xin shu xia,” 6a11 (66.14): 能毋(無)卜筮而知凶吉乎,
“Without resorting to tortoise shell and milfoil, can you foretell bad fortune from good?”
The _Hanfeizi_, V, 15/1a10 (Liao, _Han Fei Tzu_ 1:134), when listing portents of ruin, states:
“[If the ruler] indulges in selecting lucky days, worships spirits,
trusts in divination by tortoise shell and milfoil, and seeks enjoyment from sacrificial feasts,
ruin is possible.” The _Huainanzi_, 8/1a11-13 (Morgan, _Tao, the Great Luminant_, 80),
also states that in the time of great purity, “there was no recourse to the selection of lucky days,
the casting of lots, or divination using trigrams.”

是謂寬乎形[^bai-xin-29]
This means that he relaxes his body, (形 *geng*)

[^bai-xin-29]: The Yang edition writes 刑, “punishments,” for 形, “form” or “body.”
Guo Moruo would follow the Yang edition since he believes that the discussion
is primarily dealing with military affairs.

徒居而致名
And without doing anything achieves fame. (名 *mjieng*)

去[^bai-xin-30]善之言
He abjures the well-spoken word
為善之事
yet performs the well-done deed.
事成
Though his undertakings are successful,
而顧反無名
he seeks to remain nameless.
能者無名[^bai-xin-31]
Those who possess real ability do not become famous.
從事無事[^bai-xin-32]
They manage their affairs so smoothly there appears to be nothing to it.

[^bai-xin-30]: Xu Weiyu would emend 去 to 出 in accordance with the “Appended Treatise A” 繫辭
of the _Yi jing_, VII, 6/5b5-6 (Wilhelm, _I Ching_ 1:328): “The Master said,
‘The man of quality remains within his room. If the words he speaks are well spoken (出其言善),
then from even beyond a thousand *li*, people will respond to them.’”
Thus this sentence would read: “The words he speaks are well spoken; the deeds he performs are well done.”

[^bai-xin-31]: The Yang edition mistakenly writes 口 for 名.

[^bai-xin-32]: The following sentence, 審量出入而觀物所載, is clearly out of place.
I have transferred it to 9b2 (69.12).
