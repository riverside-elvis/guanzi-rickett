## 內業 Nei ye XIII

凡人之生也
It is ever so that man’s life (生 *sreng*)[^nei-ye-149]

[^nei-ye-149]: This stanza is similar in content to a paragraph in the “Xin shu xia,” 7b11-8a2 (68.4-6).

必以平正
Is certain to depend on equanimity and good judgment. (正 *tjieng*)

所以失之必以喜怒
Its loss is certain to be because of joy and anger,
憂患
sorrow and suffering.

是故止怒
Thus, for arresting anger,
莫若詩
nothing is better than poetry.

去憂
For getting rid of sorrow,
莫若樂
nothing is better than music.

節樂
For moderating music,
莫若禮
nothing is better than rules of propriety.

守禮
For preserving rules of propriety,
莫若敬
nothing is better than respect. (敬 *ki’eng*)

守敬
For preserving respect,
莫若靜
nothing is better than quiescence. (靜 *dzjieng*)[^nei-ye-150]

[^nei-ye-150]: This Confucian-sounding passage may well represent a later addition. See n. 40, above.

內靜外敬
Inwardly quiescent and outwardly respectful, (敬 *ki’eng*)

能反其性
You may revert to your true nature, (性 *sjieng*)

性將大定
And it will become completely stable. (定 *deng*)
