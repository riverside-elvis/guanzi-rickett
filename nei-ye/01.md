# 內業 Nei ye (Inner Workings)

## 內業 Nei ye I

### [I.1]

凡物之精
It is ever so that the vital essence of things
此則為生
is what gives them life. (生 *sreng*)

下生五穀
Below it gives life to the five grains[^nei-ye-60];
上為列星
above it creates the ranked stars. (星 *seng*)

[^nei-ye-60]: The five grains are usually listed as (1) hemp, two kinds of millet, wheat, and beans
or (2) rice, two kinds of millet, wheat, and pulse.

流於天地之間
When floating between heaven and earth,
謂之鬼神
we call it ghost or spirit. (神 *zdjien*)

藏於胸中
When stored in the breast,
謂之聖人
we call it sageliness. (人 *njien*)[^nei-ye-61]

[^nei-ye-61]: For a discussion and partial translations of this section
and other portions of this chapter, see Needham, _Science and Civilisation_, vols. 2 and 4 *passim*;
Graham, _Disputers of the Way_ 100-105; and Roth, “Psychology and Self-Cultivation”
and “Redaction Criticism and the Early History of Taoism.”
Perhaps the first attempt to translate the “Nei ye” into a Western language
was made by Gustav Haloun and Joseph Needham
(see Needham, “Bibliography C,” _Science and Civilisation_ 2:630),
but only the small section mentioned above was ever published.

### [I.2]

是故氣人[^nei-ye-62]
Thus, man’s vital force---

[^nei-ye-62]: Emending 民 to 人 in accordance with the Yin Commentary [Ma Feibai].
He Ruzhang and Guo Moruo would emend 民 to 名. The line would then read: “Thus, we call it ‘vital force.’”

杲乎
How bright!
如登於天
As if mounting the heavens. (天 *ten*)

杳乎
How dark!
如入於淵
As if entering an abyss. (淵 *·wen*)

綽[^nei-ye-63]乎
How vast!
如在於海
As if filling the ocean. (海 *xməy*)

[^nei-ye-63]: Reading 淖 as 綽 [Ding Shihan].

卒乎
How compact!
如在於己[^nei-ye-64]
As if contained within the self. (己 *kiəy*)

[^nei-ye-64]: Guo Moruo and Zhao Shouzheng would read 卒 as 窣 and 己 as 紀.
Thus: “How lofty! As if residing on a barren peak.” The rhyme would remain the same.

### [I.3]

是故此氣也
Thus, this vital force---

不可止以力
Never to be restrained by physical strength,
而可安以德
it may be brought to rest by spiritual Power. (德 *tək*)

不可呼以聲
Never to be summoned by one’s call,
而可迎以意[^nei-ye-65]
it may be made welcome by one’s power of awareness. (意 *·iəy*)

[^nei-ye-65]: Emending 音 (*iəm*) to 意 (*iəy*, group 之, 3rd tone),
which completes the rhyme with 德 (*tək*, group 之, 4th tone) [Yasui and Wang Niansun].

敬守勿失
Respectfully preserve and never lose it,
是謂成德
such is called perfecting one’s Power. (德 *tək*)

德成
Power being perfected,
而智出
wisdom develops,
萬物畢[^nei-ye-66]得
and all things may be fully comprehended. (得 *tək*)

[^nei-ye-66]: Emending 果 to 畢 in accordance with a similar statement
in the “Xin shu xia,” 5b11 (66.8) [Yasui and Wang Niansun].
