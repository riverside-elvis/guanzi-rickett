## 內業 Nei ye XIV

### [XIV.1]

凡食之道
It is ever so that concerning eating habits,

大充
With excessive gorging,
氣傷
the breath will be harmed (傷 *st’jiang*)

而形不藏[^nei-ye-151]
And the form will be unable to hold it all. (藏 *dzang*)

[^nei-ye-151]: The writing of 大充傷而不臧 is clearly corrupt.
I have followed Li Zheming in emending this line to 大充氣傷而形不藏.
According to the _Lüshi chunqiu_, I, 3/7b10-12 (Wilhelm, _Frühling und Herbst_, 8):
“If a person tastes a great many delicacies, his stomach will be stuffed.
When stuffed, the insides will be stretched too tightly.
When they are stretched too tightly, the breath cannot circulate.
If such a state continues for long, can life be maintained?”

大攝
With excessive abstention,
骨枯
the bones will dry up (枯 *kay*)

而血沍
And the blood will congeal. (沍 *gay*)

充攝之間
The middle ground between gorging and abstention
此謂和成
is called moderation, (成 *djieng*)

精之所舍
It provides a place for the vital essence to dwell
而知之所生
and for knowledge to develop. (生 *sreng*)

飢飽之失度
If hunger or overindulgence is excessive, (度 *day*)

乃為之圖
Deal with it in a planned manner. (圖 *day*)

飽則疾動
When too full, quickly move about;
飢則廣思
when hungry, relax your thoughts;
老則忘[^nei-ye-152]慮
when old, forget your worries. (慮 *liay*)

[^nei-ye-152]: Emending 長 to 忘, both here and below [Guo Moruo].
Ma Feibai would leave the sentence as is, reading it as:
“When old, put a premium on (i.e., be sparing in) your mental effort.” See n. 155, below.

飽不疾動
If, having eaten too much, you do not quickly move about,

氣不通於四末
The breath will not circulate to your four extremities. (末 *mwat*)

飢不廣思
If, being hungry, you do not relax your thoughts,

飢[^nei-ye-153]而不廢[^nei-ye-154]
Your hunger will not be alleviated. (廢 *pjwar*)

[^nei-ye-153]: Emending 飽 to 飢 [Yu Yue].

[^nei-ye-154]: Rhyme: 廢 (group 祭, 3rd tone) with 末 ( group 祭, 4th tone), above,
and 竭 (group 祭, 4th tone), below.

老不忘慮困乃遬竭
If, being old, you do not forget your worries, when troubled,[^nei-ye-155] you will be quickly exhausted. (竭 *giat*)

[^nei-ye-155]: As mentioned above, Ma Feibai would leave 長 in the previous sentence as is,
but he would emend 困 here to 囦 = 淵. Thus this sentence would read:
“If being old, you do not put a premium on your mental effort,
the wellspring will be quickly exhausted.”

### [XIV.2]

大心
Expand your mind,
而敞[^nei-ye-156]
and you will feel release. (敞 *t’jang*)

[^nei-ye-156]: Emending 敢 to 敞 [He Ruzhang].

寬氣
Deepen your breathing,
而廣
and you will feel relaxed. (廣 *kwang*)

其形安
Your form will be at ease
而不移
and never restless. (移 *ria*)

能守一
You will be able to focus your power of awareness,
而棄萬苛
and dispose of the myriad minor irritations. (苛 *ga*)

見利
On seeing profit,
不誘
you will not be enticed. (誘 *yriwəy*)

見害
On seeing harm,
不懼[^nei-ye-157]
you will not be frightened. (懼 *gjway*)

[^nei-ye-157]: Irregular rhyme: 懼 (group 魚) with 誘 (group 之), above.

寬舒而仁
Being relaxed and humane,
獨樂其身
you will find happiness within yourself. (身 *st’jien*)

是謂運[^nei-ye-158]氣
This is called setting in motion the vital force
意行似天
so that your awareness and action become like Heaven. (*t’en*)

[^nei-ye-158]: Reading 雲 as 云 = 運 [Yasui and Guo Moruo].
