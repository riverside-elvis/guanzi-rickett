## 內業 Nei ye IX

### [IX.1]

人能正靜
When man is capable of good judgment and remaining quiescent,[^nei-ye-120]
皮膚裕寬
his flesh will be plump and fall,
耳目聰明
his ears and eyes sharp and clear. (明 *miwang*)

[^nei-ye-120]: For a somewhat abbreviated version of this portion of stanza IX,
see the “Xin shu xia,” 7a11-7b2 (67.12-14).

筋伸[^nei-ye-121]
His muscles will become taut,
而骨強
and his bones sturdy. (強 *gjang*)

[^nei-ye-121]: The parallel sentence in the “Xin shu xia,” 7a11 (67.12), for 信 (=伸) writes 肕, “firm.”

乃能戴大圜[^nei-ye-122]
Thus he is able to wear on his head the Great Circle[^nei-ye-123]
而履大方
and plant his feet on the Great Square, (方 *pjwang*)

[^nei-ye-122]: The “Xin shu xia,” 7a11 (67.13), for 圜 writes 圓.

[^nei-ye-123]: The “Great Circle” refers to Heaven and the “Great Square” to Earth.

鑒[^nei-ye-124]於大清
Find his reflection in the Great Purity
視於大明
and be comparable to the great luminaries. (明 *miwang*)[^nei-ye-125]

[^nei-ye-124]: The “Xin shu xia,” 7a12 (67.13), for 鑒 writes 鏡,
as does the text cited below. The translation remains the same.

[^nei-ye-125]: The _Huainanzi_, 2/4a1-3 (Morgan, _Tao, the Great Luminant_, 36),
contains a somewhat similar passage in reference to the sage:
是故能戴大貝者履大方，鏡太清者視大明，立太平者處大堂
“For this reason, being one who is able to wear on his head the Great Circle,
he plants his feet on the Great Square, finding his reflection in the Great Purity,
he is comparable to the great luminaries, establishing great peace,
he occupies the great hall.” “he “Great Purity” is the Way;
the “great luminaries” are the sun and moon.
For further discussion, see the “Xin shu xia,” n. 32.

### [IX.2]

敬慎
Respectful and cautious,
無忒
and avoiding excesses,
日新其德
he daily renews his Power. (德 *tək*)

徧知天下
He comes to understand everything in the world
窮於四極
and thoroughly examines its four extremities. (極 *giək*)

敬發其充
The respectful development of his inner well-being
是謂內得
is what we call internalization [of the Spirit]. (得 *tək*)

然而不反
Being like this and never retrogressing,
此生無[^nei-ye-126]忒
his is a life without excesses. (忒 *tək*)

[^nei-ye-126]: The meaning of these final two lines is not clear.
I have followed Guo Moruo in emending the next to the last character, 之, to 亡 = 無.
