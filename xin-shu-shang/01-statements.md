# 心術上 Xin Shu Shang (Art of the Mind, Part I)

## [Statement I]

心之在體君之位也
In the body, the mind holds the position of the prince.
九竅之有職官之分也
The functions of the nine apertures resemble the separate responsibilities of officials.[^xin-shu-shang-13]

[^xin-shu-shang-13]: *Gwan* 官, “office,” or “office holder,” also can mean “sense organ.”
 Thus the _Xunzi_, XI, 17/10a9-10 (Knoblock, _Xunzi_ 3:16), states:
“The heart/mind occupies the central cavity in order to control the five sense organs.
Now for this reason it is referred to as the natural prince.”
The nine apertures are the eyes, ears, nostrils, mouth, anus, and sex organ.

心處其道
If the mind is at rest in the Way, (道 *dəw*)

九竅循理[^xin-shu-shang-14]
The nine apertures will function properly. (理 *liəy*)

[^xin-shu-shang-14]: Irregular rhyme: 道 (group 幽, 4th tone) with 理 (group 之, second tone).

欲充盈[^xin-shu-shang-15]
Should lust and desire occupy it to the full, (盈 *·ieng*)

[^xin-shu-shang-15]: Emending 益 to 盈 to complete the rhyme with 聲, “sounds” [Wang Niansun].

目不見色
The eyes will not see colors;
耳不聞聲
the ears will not hear sounds. (聲 *·st’ieng*)

故曰[^xin-shu-shang-16]
Therefore it is said:

[^xin-shu-shang-16]: Igai, Dai Wang, and Yu Yue would delete 故曰,
“Therefore it is said,” as an interpolation taken from the explanation (3a1; 63.13).
Guo Moruo argues that the two characters indicate that this statement
is a quotation from some other text and should be retained.

上離其道
“If the person on high departs from the Way, (道 *dəw*)

下失其事[^xin-shu-shang-17]
Those below will be lax in their work.” (事 *dziəy*)

[^xin-shu-shang-17]: Irregular rhyme: 道 (group 幽, 4th tone) with 事 (group 之, 3rd tone).
He Ruzhang would insert here a line from the explanation (3a 1-2; 63.13):
心術者無為而制疲者也, “The art of the mind lies in controlling the apertures through nonassertiveness.”
I would question this emendation because such a sentence, coming at the end of the statement,
appears as a non sequitur and violates the established rhyme pattern.
The only logical place for it would be following the second sentence concerning the nine appertures,
but this does not accord with the sequence of the ideas presented in the explanation.
For further discussion of this problem, see my introductory comments and n. 36, below.

## [Statement II]

毋代馬走
Do not attempt to do the running for a horse;
使盡其力
let it exhaust its strength. (力 *liək*)

毋代鳥飛
Do not attempt to do the flying for a bird;
使獘其羽翼
let it wear out its wings. (翼 *riək*)

## [Statement III]

毋先物動
Do not be the first to make a move
以觀其則
so that you may observe patterns of action (則 *tsək*)

動
If you move,
則失位
you will lose your position;
靜
if you remain quiet,
乃自得
you will naturally retain it. (得 *tək*)

## [Statement IV]

道不遠
The Way is not distant,
而難極也
yet it is difficult to reach. (極 *giək*)

與人並處
It exists here among men,
而難得也
yet it is difficult to comprehend. (得 *tək*)

## [Statement V]

虛其欲
Become empty of desires,
神將入舍
and the Spirit will enter to take up its abode. (舍 *st’iay*)

掃除不潔[^xin-shu-shang-18]
Should you fail to make a clean sweep,
神不[^xin-shu-shang-19]留處
the Spirit will not remain. (處 *t’jay*)

[^xin-shu-shang-18]: The Yang edition for 潔 writes 絜. The meaning remains the same.

[^xin-shu-shang-19]: Emending 乃 to 不 in accordance with the Zhu edition [Dai Wang, Ding Shihan, and Yu Yue].

## [Statement VI]

皆欲智
All men desire wisdom,
而莫索其所以智[^xin-shu-shang-20]
but no one understands the means to obtain it.
智乎
Wisdom!
智乎
Wisdom!
投之海外無自奪
Cast it beyond the seas to avoid becoming its captive.
求之者不及虛[^xin-shu-shang-21]之者
Those who seek it cannot compare with those who are vacuous.
夫聖[^xin-shu-shang-22]人無求之也
Now the sage does not seek it.
故能虛無
Therefore he is able to be vacuous.

[^xin-shu-shang-20]: Deleting 乎 as an interpolation from the following line [Wang Niansun and Guo Moruo].

[^xin-shu-shang-21]: Emending 得處 to 及虛 [Guo Moruo].
[Both terms are in reverse order in the original text, so I've corrected them. --RSE]

[^xin-shu-shang-22]: Emending 正 to 聖 [Wang Niansun].

## [Statement VII]

虛而[^xin-shu-shang-23]無形謂之道
What is vacuous and formless is called the Way. (道 *dəw*)[^xin-shu-shang-24]

[^xin-shu-shang-23]: Emending 無虛無 to 虛而 [Wang hiansun].
The Li Shan 李善 commentary to the _Wen xuan_, 20/13a13 and 29/11a8, cites this line, writing: 虛無形謂之道.

[^xin-shu-shang-24]: The “Dao fa” 道法 section of the Mawangdui *Jing fa*, 1, in speaking about the Way, states:
“Vacuous and formless, its connecting thread is truly profound, and it is that which gives life to all things.”

化育萬物謂之德[^xin-shu-shang-25]
What transforms and nourishes all things is called the Power. (德 *tək*)

[^xin-shu-shang-25]: Irregular rhyme: 道 (group 幽, 3rd tone) with 德 (group 之, 4th tone).

## [Statement VIII]

君臣
What governs relations between prince and minister,
父子
father and son,
人間之事謂之義
and man and man is called duty (*yi* 義).
登降
What governs ascending or descending the hall,
揖讓
bowing and yielding to others,
貴賤有等
degrees of honor and lowliness,
親疏之體
and distinctions between near and distant kin,
謂之禮
are called rules of propriety (禮 *li*).

## [Statement IX]

簡物小未一道[^xin-shu-shang-26]殺僇禁誅謂之法
What determines execution, extermination, prohibition, or punishment
for even the smallest of things that are not at one with the Way
is called the law (*fa* 法).

[^xin-shu-shang-26]: The meaning of 簡物小未一道 is not at all clear.
I have generally followed Yasui’s interpretation. Zhao Shouzheng has followed Guo Moruo,
who says 物 is balanced with 簡 and means “complex” as opposed to “simple.”
He would also emend 未 to 大. Thus Zhao would translate this sentence as
“What makes use of the Way to equalize the simple and complex
and determines execution, extermination, prohibition, or punishment is called law.”

## [Statement X]

大道可安
The great Way can bring about peace,
而不可說
but it cannot be described in words.

## [Statement XI]

真[^xin-shu-shang-27]人之言不儀[^xin-shu-shang-28]不侧[^xin-shu-shang-29]
The true man’s words are unprejudiced and impartial. (侧 *tsiək*)

[^xin-shu-shang-27]: Emending 直, “straightforward,” to 真 [Igai and Wang Niansun].

[^xin-shu-shang-28]: Reading 義 as 儀 [Zhang Peilun and Zhang Binglin].

[^xin-shu-shang-29]: Emending 顧 to 侧 in order to preserve the rhyme with 色 and 則 [Zhang Peilun].

不出於口不見於色
Nothing issues from his mouth or appears on his face. (色 *siək*)

四海之人又庸知其則
Who within the four seas can know his pattern of action? (則 *tsək*)

## [Statement XII]

天曰虛
Heaven is said to be vacuous;
地曰靜
Earth is said to be quiescent.
乃不貸[^xin-shu-shang-30]
Thus there are no miscalculations.

[^xin-shu-shang-30]: Emending 伐 to 貸 [Yu Yue].
貸 (*t’əy*) rhymes with 側, 色, and 則 in the previous statement.
Thus, it is possible that this passage should be included there rather than stand alone.

## [Statement XIII]

潔其宮開其門
Cleanse the mansion of your mind and open your gates of perception! (門 *mwən*)

去私毋言
Once you have rid yourself of selfish desires and stopped talking,
神明若存
the Spirit will appear. (存 *dzwən*)

## [Statement XIV]

紛乎其若亂
When matters become confused and appear to be in turmoil,
靜之
remain quiescent toward them,
而自治
and they will put themselves in order. (治 *diəy*)

強不能遍立
Force cannot always establish things;
智不能盡謀
wisdom cannot plan for every exigency. (謀 *mjwəy*)

## [Statement XV]

物固有形
Things have fixed forms;
形固有名
forms have fixed names. (名 *mjieng*)

名當謂之聖人[^xin-shu-shang-31]
He who makes names fit realities is called a sage. (人 *njien*)

[^xin-shu-shang-31]: Irregular rhyme: 名 (group 耕) with 人 (group 真).

故必知不言之言[^xin-shu-shang-32]
Therefore one must understand the unspoken word
無為之事
and be nonassertive in affairs. (事 *dziəy*)

[^xin-shu-shang-32]: Following the interpretation of the explanation (4b9; 65.7),
which adds 之言 after 不言 [Wang Niansun].

然後知道之紀
Thereafter one may come to know the Way’s guidelines. (紀 *kiəy*)

## [Statement XVI]

殊形異埶
There are different forms and different situations,
不與萬物異理
but the sage never deviates from things’ inherent order. (理 *liəy*)

故可以為天下始[^xin-shu-shang-33]
Therefore, in the world, he is considered first among all. (始 *stiəy*)

[^xin-shu-shang-33]: The Yang edition mistakenly omits 始 [Zhang Peilun and Xu Weiyu].

## [Statement XVII]

人之可殺
Men may be executed
以其惡死也
because they dislike death. (死 *sjier*)

其可不利
They may be deprived of profit
以其好利也
because they like profit. (利 *lier*)

是以君子不怵[^xin-shu-shang-34]乎好
Thus, the man of quality is not to be enticed by something he likes
不迫乎惡
nor oppressed by what he dislikes (惡 *·ak*)

[^xin-shu-shang-34]: Following the Yang edition and Ming print of the Zhao edition,
both of which for *xiu* 休 write *chu* 怵. The explanation (5a6; 65.12) also writes *chu*.
The Yin commentary originally wrote *xiu*. (In the surviving Yang print
the commentary has been altered to read *chu* to conform with the text).
The Yin commentary explains *xiu* as meaning 止, “to cease” or “to halt.”
Thus the phrase would read: “The man of quality is not to be deterred by something he likes.”

恬愉無為
He is contented and nonassertive,
去智與故[^xin-shu-shang-35]
rejecting wisdom and pretense. (故 *kay*)

[^xin-shu-shang-35]: Rhyme: 惡 (group 魚, 4th tone) with 故 (group 魚, 3rd tone).
According to Xu Weiyu, 故 should be read as 詐 (*tsray*, group 魚, 3rd tone).

## [Statement XVIII]

其應也
When responding to things,
非所設也
he does not have any preconceptions.
其動也
When making a move,
非所取也
he does not jump to conclusions.

## [Statement XIX]

過在自用
Mistakes are inherent in relying on one’s own opinions.
罪在變化
Crimes are inherent in forcing change.
是故
For this reason,
有道之君
the prince who adheres to the Way,
其處也
when at rest,
若無知
appears to lack knowledge;
其應物也
when responding to things,
若偶之
appears to be at one with them.
靜因之道也
This is the way of quiescence and relying on things as they are.
